# Hormozi Proposal Review Rubric

Use this rubric to review proposals, landing pages, sales decks, pricing pages, screenshots, and mixed folders.

Score each major item:
- 0 = weak / unknown
- 1 = partial / inconsistent
- 2 = strong / proven

You may optionally translate this into a 10-point narrative score in the final writeup, but the underlying logic should stay tied to these criteria.

## 1. Market

Questions:
- Is the pain urgent, expensive, embarrassing, risky, or growth-blocking?
- Can the buyer be clearly identified and reached?
- Does the buyer already spend money on this category or adjacent alternatives?
- Is the segment growing or pulled by a tailwind?
- Can the buyer describe the desired outcome clearly?

Red flag:
- If the market fails two or more of these filters, the proposal likely has a market problem, not just a copy problem.

## 2. Offer / Value Equation

Core equation:

```text
Perceived Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

Assess each lever.

### Dream outcome
Strong when:
- the outcome is specific
- the outcome is meaningful emotionally, financially, or strategically
- the proposal makes the upside feel concrete

Weak when:
- the promise is vague
- the outcome sounds generic or commodity-like
- the buyer cannot tell what actually improves

### Perceived likelihood
Strong when:
- there is relevant proof
- there is a believable mechanism
- the offer includes diagnostics, fit criteria, or trust-building process

Weak when:
- it asks the buyer to believe a large claim with little proof
- proof is generic, irrelevant, or missing

### Time delay
Strong when:
- there is a fast path to first value
- onboarding is short and clear
- there are early wins, templates, setup help, or quick-start assets

Weak when:
- time to value is undefined
- the proposal asks for a big commitment before any visible result

### Effort and sacrifice
Strong when:
- the proposal reduces work, fear, switching cost, confusion, or implementation burden
- support is concrete and useful

Weak when:
- the customer must do too much heavy lifting
- the process sounds tiring, ambiguous, or operationally painful

## 3. Offer Engineering

### Objection handling
Check whether likely objections are turned into offer components.
Examples:
- "I don't know where to start" → onboarding, checklist, setup help
- "I don't trust this will work for me" → proof, qualification, examples
- "I don't have time" → templates, automation, done-with-you or done-for-you help
- "This feels risky" → clear scope, pilot, guarantee, milestone structure

### Offer stack quality
Strong when the stack increases:
- likelihood of success
- speed to value
- ease of implementation
- clarity of outcome

Weak when the stack is inflated with random extras.

### Bonus quality
A bonus should remove a real blocker.
Call it weak if it does not increase:
- perceived likelihood
- reduced time delay
- reduced effort / sacrifice

### Scarcity / urgency
Only valid when tied to real constraints.
Flag fake scarcity aggressively.

### Guarantee / risk reversal
Strong when:
- success is defined clearly
- the seller can influence the outcome
- customer responsibilities are clear
- abuse risk is controlled
- the guarantee attracts serious buyers

Weak when:
- it promises fantasies
- it is vague or easily abused
- it damages margins or trust

### Naming / positioning
Strong when the name signals:
- audience and/or mechanism
- result
- timeframe or scope
- package type

Default rule:
- clarity beats cleverness

## 4. Pricing and Delivery

Questions:
- Is the proposal priced high enough to support quality delivery?
- Does pricing communicate seriousness?
- Could payment terms solve resistance without lowering total price?
- Can the business fulfill the promised result profitably?
- Is expensive human help reserved for the moments that matter most?

Call out:
- underpricing driven by fear
- delivery promises that break margin
- pricing disconnected from the effort needed to produce outcomes

## 5. Proof and Credibility

Look for:
- case studies
- quantified outcomes
- before/after evidence
- credibility markers
- fit criteria
- testimonials with relevance, not just praise

Weak proof patterns:
- social proof with no similarity to the buyer
- testimonials with no outcomes
- big claims with zero evidence

## 6. CTA and Funnel Logic

Questions:
- Is the next step obvious?
- Does the CTA match the temperature of the offer?
- Does the page/proposal reduce friction to take the next step?
- Is the requested commitment larger than the promise justifies?

Weak CTA patterns:
- buried CTA
- generic "book a call" with no reason
- next step that feels too heavy, too vague, or too risky

## 7. Lead Generation Readiness

Only score this if lead gen is part of the proposal or context.

### Lead magnet
Strong when it is:
- narrow
- fast
- specific
- easy to consume
- naturally connected to the paid offer
- proof-building

Weak when it is a generic PDF, vague webinar, bloated report, or newsletter CTA.

### Channel readiness
Check whether the proposal has enough evidence to justify:
- warm outreach
- content
- cold outreach
- paid ads

Do not recommend scaling paid ads or multi-channel expansion until:
- offer has conversion evidence
- landing page / CTA path works
- follow-up process exists
- unit economics are visible
- fulfillment capacity exists

### Rule-of-100 execution discipline
Prefer one clearly defined acquisition action executed consistently enough to generate signal.

## 8. Measurement Maturity

Look for visibility into:
- close rate
- average selling price
- gross margin
- CAC
- LTGP/LTV
- payback period
- time to first value
- activation/onboarding completion
- refund or guarantee claims
- channel-specific response/conversion rates

If measurement is absent, call out that scaling decisions are being made on vibes.

## 9. Interpretation Bands

Total the core scorecard if enough information exists:
- 0–12: do not scale; rebuild market, offer, proof, and delivery
- 13–22: validate; refine proof, delivery, and founder-led acquisition
- 23–30: scale carefully; increase volume in the best channel and monitor fulfillment quality

## 10. Review Discipline

For every major criticism:
1. Point to the exact element being criticized
2. Explain why it weakens conversion or trust
3. Tie it to one of the rubric principles
4. Suggest a stronger alternative

Default diagnosis order:
- market
- offer
- proof
- delivery
- lead flow
- sales execution

Anti-hype rule:
- Better offers beat louder claims.
- Trust compounds; tricks decay.
